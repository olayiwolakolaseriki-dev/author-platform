The Borrowed Map

Navigating Faith, Ambition, and the Foreign Scripts of Modern Africa

For generations, the roadmap of African ambition has been shaped by foreign contours—from colonial administrative curricula to Western corporate archetypes and imported theological systems.

In The Borrowed Map, Olayiwola Kola-Seriki traces the profound disorientation that occurs when external validation becomes the sole barometer of personal and societal progress, laying out a rigorous call to rediscover our own coordinates.

“The map you inherited is not the territory. Draw your own.”

The Central Thesis

What does it mean to arrive when the destination was never yours?

From Lagos to London, Nairobi to New York, young Africans are achieving unprecedented prominence across global industries. Yet beneath the accolades lies a pervasive existential restlessness: an acute awareness that success is still largely audited by Western institutional approval, and that departure remains the dominant definition of arrival.

The Borrowed Map interrogates three foundational pillars:

1. The Foreign Scripts of Ambition

How post-colonial education systems and global capitalist aspirations quietly estrange African talent from domestic institutional investment.

2. Faith at the Crossroads

How Christian theology on the continent was influenced by transactional prosperity dogmas and Western cultural packaging, and how genuine faith can be reclaimed.

3. Re-drawing the Native Compass

A constructive architecture for authentic self-determination, generational stewardship, and homegrown institutions.

Written with philosophical depth and cultural intimacy, the book speaks directly to ambitious Africans in the homeland and diaspora, as well as anyone concerned with the moral dimensions of post-colonial development.

Author's Perspective

Why I Wrote The Borrowed Map

I wrote this book because I watched some of the most gifted minds of my generation achieve everything our parents, schools, and foreign institutions demanded of them—only to arrive at a profound spiritual exhaustion.

We had mastered the curricula. We had learned to translate our ideas into the acceptable vocabularies of Western boardrooms, think tanks, and academic faculties. But in doing so, we had silently accepted an unspoken premise: that to become significant, we had to leave ourselves behind.

The Borrowed Map is not an exercise in bitterness or isolationism. It is a work of gratitude for the journey, coupled with the courage to say: the map we were handed is insufficient for where we must go next. It is written for anyone who has ever felt the silent dislocation of succeeding in a system that was never designed with their spiritual and cultural flourishing in mind.

— Olayiwola “Layi” Kola-Seriki

The Borrowed Map · Sample Excerpt

Chapter One: The Cartography of Arrival

By Olayiwola Kola-Seriki · Forthcoming

We were taught to navigate the terrain long before we ever questioned who had drawn the borders. The map was presented not as an argument or a contingent artifact of history, but as an undeniable gospel: go to school, master the foreign grammar, outrun the chaos of the immediate, and secure a life that would be legible to institutions ten thousand kilometers away.

It was an intoxicating proposition because it promised safety in an unpredictable world. Yet, as so many of us reached those fabled checkpoints of modern success, an unsettling silence set in. We held the credentials, we had mastered the dialects of global corporate boardrooms and prestigious fellowships, yet we felt a persistent spiritual displacement. The map had worked, but it had led us into a landscape where our deepest longings had no name.

To interrogate a borrowed map is not an act of cynicism; it is the first prerequisite of self-respect. It demands that we ask: what parts of our souls did we leave behind at the border crossings? Can faith survive when it is severed from indigenous reality? And how does an emerging generation re-author its own destiny on its own soil?
